Minimal Circuit C2

The smallest circuit found so far that learns, in its life, which colour is poison, and passes the full test: held-out lives, every part off by about 3%, and half, usual and double noise. It spins to search, drives straight at food, and lets poison slide past.

How it works

  1. The clock spins it. M1 fires on its own about 230 times per 1,000 ticks (226 at half noise, 257 at double): its rate is set by its own parts, not the noise. One thruster alone pushes and turns, so with nothing in view the creature circles and its eyes sweep all round.
  2. Food sends it straight. When an eye sees the colour the bit says is food, its pulses kick M0, the second thruster fires too, and the creature drives at the block.
  3. Poison slides past. The other colour kicks nothing, so the creature keeps circling and the block leaves its view.
  4. The bit. m is a capacitor with no leak. Each read element takes m as its input and an eye as its gate: one passes S0's pulses when m is high, the other S1's when m is low. At birth m is ½, so both colours half-kick M0 and the creature approaches everything.
  5. Learning. A good taste opens both write elements for a moment. Each writes m its own way, in proportion to how recently its eye fired: the sensor node's own decay, about 2 ticks, is the memory of what was just seen. The eye that saw the meal wins, and m moves towards it.

Per 1,000 ticks, 64 held-out seeds × 2 (red, then blue, as poison), every parameter off by about 3% at birth. Score: food − 2 × poison − 2 × (food short of 1). The job: food at least 1 and food:poison at least 3 at all three noise levels. For scale, the hand-designed circuit (78 parts) fails it under the same mismatch.

Still open

  • Three tuned values sit at their search bounds: the clock's reset gain (−20) and time constant (100), and the taste node's rest (−3.0). The optimiser wanted to go further.
  • Not yet checked at a finer integration step in the real simulator.
  • The lower-bound argument says 21 parts. Two 21-part variants are being tuned now, and an open evolutionary search will test the argument itself.
